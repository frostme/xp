## Four observed patterns

1. Saving the identity and variants gives the operator a stable place to review before launch.
2. The editor previews configured shares and explains its 100% validation at the point of commitment.
3. Pause explains the immediate serving effect and the fate of collected data. Ending asks a separate question about shipping a variant.
4. Each variant has a named denominator in the exposure panel. The result view gives the outcome its own metric and baseline.

**Weakness, inferred from the workflow.** With an existing flag, the experiment wizard shows its variants but sends the operator to the flag page to change the definition. That handoff adds a second editing surface to a simple distribution review. [Creating an experiment, “Experiment variants”](https://posthog.com/docs/experiments/creating-an-experiment#experiment-variants)

## Two transfers to xp

- Send creation directly to the saved experiment. For `checkout-copy`, say “Awaiting launch” and “Serving application default.” Show off at 100% and each treatment at 0%. At launch, preview the proposed shares. When adjusting a running split, explain that new joins use the new percentages and existing members keep their cohort. This last guarantee comes from xp’s sticky storage contract.
- Keep “Current memberships” alongside each cohort and “New assignment share” separately labeled. A serving summary can show a final winner of off above counts of 18 off, 7 short and 0 detailed. The winner confirmation can explain why these three values survive finalization. On a count error, show the failure and a retry. Only a successful read can show zero.

**Outside scope.** The chance-to-win, lift and significance views require exposure and outcome data. No ranking or suggested winner can be derived from xp’s current membership counts. [Analyzing results, “Metric results and determining significance”](https://posthog.com/docs/experiments/analyzing-results#metric-results-and-determining-significance)
